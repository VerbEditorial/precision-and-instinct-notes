The Disappearance of Jimmy Hoffa

Open Case No one has ever been charged in Hoffa's disappearance, and every person ever publicly named as a participant is now deceased. In July 2026, Hoffa's son and daughter publicly asked FBI Director Kash Patel to release the Bureau's files and name those responsible. This page does not resolve that dispute, and neither did the episode.

This page lists the sources behind the claims made in this episode, tiered by how well-established each one is. Confirmed facts come from court records, police reports, or multiple independent contemporaneous news sources. Disputed items have conflicting accounts. Single-source claims rest on one account with no corroboration — treat them accordingly.

Confirmed Facts

10 items
Confirmed
Jimmy Hoffa disappeared on July 30, 1975, after leaving his Lake Orion, Michigan home for an expected meeting at the Machus Red Fox restaurant in Bloomfield Township. He called his wife from the restaurant that afternoon, saying the man he was there to meet hadn't shown, and told her he'd be home by 4 p.m. His car was found unattended in the restaurant parking lot the next morning; he was never seen again.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Confirmed
At the time of his disappearance, Hoffa was fighting a Nixon-imposed restriction on returning to Teamsters leadership before 1980, seeking to reclaim the union presidency from incumbent Frank Fitzsimmons, and was in an open feud with New Jersey Teamsters boss and Genovese-family caporegime Anthony "Tony Pro" Provenzano — the man whose associate, Anthony Giacalone, Hoffa was at the restaurant to meet.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Confirmed
Three weeks after Hoffa vanished, police dogs alerted to his scent in a 1975 Mercury Marquis that Hoffa's foster son, Charles "Chuckie" O'Brien, had borrowed that day; a shotgun and loose ammunition were found in the car.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Confirmed
In December 1975, a federal investigator testified under oath that three men associated with Provenzano — Salvatore Briguglio, Gabriel Briguglio, and Thomas Andretta — participated in Hoffa's abduction and murder. The testimony relied substantially on the cooperation of mob associate Ralph Picardo. No charges were ever filed against any of the three, and all three denied involvement for the rest of their lives.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Confirmed
In 1976, the FBI's internal 56-page "Hoffex" memo concluded that organized-crime figures ordered Hoffa's killing over his threat to their control of the Teamsters pension fund, and identified O'Brien as the likely driver of the car with Hoffa in the back seat.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Confirmed
In 2001, FBI forensic testing matched a hair from Hoffa's own hairbrush to a hair recovered from the back seat of the Mercury — with the Bureau's own caveat that Hoffa could have ridden in the car on a different day.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Confirmed
Numerous physical searches for Hoffa's remains — a Waterford Township site in 1975, a horse farm in 2006, a Roseville driveway in 2012, an Oakland County property in 2013, and beneath the Pulaski Skyway near a former New Jersey landfill in 2021–2022 — have all concluded without finding remains or, per FBI statements, anything "of evidentiary value."
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Confirmed
Hoffa was declared legally dead in 1982. No one has ever been charged in connection with his death, and the case remains officially open.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Confirmed
In 2004, author Charles Brandt's book "I Heard You Paint Houses" published mob associate Frank Sheeran's deathbed claim that he shot Hoffa inside a Detroit house; the book was adapted into Martin Scorsese's 2019 Netflix film "The Irishman." Independent journalists and legal scholars who reviewed Sheeran's account afterward found that blood evidence at the house he named did not match Hoffa and that no physical evidence corroborates his confession.
Source: Wikipedia — I Heard You Paint Houses; Wikipedia — The Irishman
Confirmed
In 2020, Fox Nation's docuseries "Riddle: The Search for James R. Hoffa" revisited a New Jersey landfill lead, and the production's own ground-penetrating radar survey detected what appeared to be barrel-shaped metal at the site. The FBI subsequently conducted a sealed-warrant search there in 2021–2022 and reported finding "nothing of evidentiary value."
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa

Disputed / Theories

4 items
Disputed
Who ordered or carried out the killing. No consensus exists among a Provenzano-crew abduction (the 1975 sworn testimony, never tested at trial), Frank Sheeran's 2004 deathbed confession (substantially challenged by independent reporting and legal scholarship), Richard Kuklinski's posthumous jailhouse claims (called "a hoax" by a former FBI agent), and mob figure Michael Franzese's on-camera claim to know the shooter's identity without naming him.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Disputed
Gabriel Briguglio's culpability specifically. Briguglio denied involvement on camera in 2023, calling the 1975 testimony fabricated; former Detroit Organized Crime Strike Force chief Keith Corbett and longtime crime reporter Scott Burnstein have each separately said publicly they don't believe he was involved, against the original sworn testimony naming him. Briguglio died in November 2025, still neither charged nor cleared.
Source: 2023 Fox News/Fox Nation interview; reporting by journalist Dan Moldea on Briguglio's November 2025 death (link verification pending — see Flagged for Review)
Disputed
Chuckie O'Brien's role. One theory holds O'Brien was an unwitting driver used to lure Hoffa into the car; others, including former prosecutor Keith Corbett, have argued O'Brien was considered too unreliable by organized crime to be trusted with the killing itself. O'Brien died in 2020 still denying involvement.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Disputed
Where Hoffa's remains ended up. Theories include the New Jersey landfill site searched by the FBI in 2021–2022, a Michigan horse farm, a shallow grave in Oakland County, cremation at a Detroit-area facility, and a persistent urban legend that he was buried beneath the end zone of the old Giants Stadium — a claim tested and unsupported by MythBusters, and again checked and unsupported at the stadium's 2010 demolition. None of these has ever been confirmed.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa

Single-Source Claims

5 items
Single source
Frank Sheeran's entire confession, as relayed in "I Heard You Paint Houses" — no independent corroboration exists, and blood evidence at the house he named does not match Hoffa.
Source: Wikipedia — I Heard You Paint Houses
Single source
Richard Kuklinski's posthumously published jailhouse claims of participating in a four-man hit team — called a hoax by a former FBI agent.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Single source
Michael Franzese's on-camera claims to know the shooter's identity and the body's location — no name and no location precision was ever given.
Source: recorded interviews, 2018–2019 (link verification pending — see Flagged for Review)
Single source
Reputed mobster Tony Zerilli's 2013 claim that Hoffa was buried in a shallow grave later possibly relocated — a single informant's account never confirmed by the related FBI dig.
Source: Wikipedia — Jimmy Hoffa
Single source
Fox Nation's 2020 ground-penetrating radar detection of "barrel-shaped" metal at the New Jersey landfill site — one production team's equipment read, not a forensic or law-enforcement finding, and not confirmed as human remains by the FBI's follow-up search.
Source: Fox Nation, "Riddle: The Search for James R. Hoffa" (2020) (link verification pending — see Flagged for Review)
